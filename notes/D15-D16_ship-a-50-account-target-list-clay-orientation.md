# D15-D16: Ship a 50-Account Target List + Clay Orientation (2026-09-17)

## TL;DR
- The homework was misread by several students — it required a **company/account list**, not a people list; "account" and "company" are synonymous in GTM engineering, reinforced from D09-D10.
- Two hard Clay house rules for the next 3-6 months: **auto-run stays OFF** everywhere except webhook (live) tables, and **never delete a column — always Hide/Unhide** (deleted data costs 50 credits to recover and sometimes can't be recovered at all).
- Clay's own native company/people data is unreliable (scraped LinkedIn data with fake profiles); always start account qualification from a real data provider (Apollo/Prospeo) — never search companies natively in Clay.
- For AI-agent enrichment steps: use the "Prompter" to auto-generate structured prompts (context/objective/instructions/examples), output as JSON schema, and pick **Helium** for any web-search task (fallback Neon, then Argan — Argan costs 3 credits so avoid unless needed); trust only green (high-confidence) AI outputs, not orange/red.
- Messaging must use signals as color/context, never recite them back verbatim (e.g., never say "we saw you raised funding" or "we saw you're hiring 10 salespeople") — verify signals are actually true before using them (a real anecdote: a "recent funding" signal was wrong because it belonged to an acquired subsidiary, not the target company).

## Key concepts
**Account list standardization**: The task was "ship a 50-account target list" — everyone initially built either individual-people lists or overly detailed company sheets; Yogesh corrected this twice (Sampath, Alok). The final required format per company row: **Company Name, Company Domain, Company LinkedIn URL** — nothing else at this stage (strip out industry, headcount, etc., before importing to Clay, since Clay will enrich all of that natively). Sheet should be renamed "company sheet" before import. In real GTM engineering this 50 scales to 500-1,000 companies — the 50 is just a training-sized sample.

**Company-first vs. person-first qualification order**: Rithika asked why the process starts at company level rather than searching people directly in Clay. Yogesh's answer: company-level qualification (funding, hiring, signals) happens first to decide *if an account is even worth reaching*, before spending effort finding people at it. Also, Clay's own native "search company" feature is unreliable — Clay scrapes LinkedIn, and LinkedIn is full of fake/inflated company profiles (anyone can list themselves as "500-1,000 employee AI company" with a non-working domain). Real, validated company data should always come from Apollo/Prospeo (or similar), then be enriched further inside Clay — never search companies from scratch inside Clay.

**Clay's core mental model**: Clay is an "orchestration platform" — if a capability isn't native to Clay, Clay has partnered ("waterfall") with third-party providers so the capability is still accessible from inside Clay. A "workbook" in Clay is like a Google Sheets file with multiple tabs; a "folder" holds multiple workbooks (used per client). Two entry modes: import a CSV (for one-off/manual work) or set up a **webhook table** (a live, always-on automated table used when many people/teams don't want to keep re-opening/re-running things manually).

**Company-identifier vs. person-identifier requirements**: Any company-level enrichment needs a company domain or company LinkedIn URL as the identifier; any person-level enrichment needs the person's LinkedIn URL. This determines what minimum columns must exist before you can run any enrichment.

**Testing discipline in Clay**: Every enrichment run defaults to either 10 rows or "all rows" — Clay deliberately does not let you test on a single row, because a single lucky/unlucky row won't reveal whether a prompt/enrichment config actually works. Always test on the first 10 rows before scaling to the full list.

**Signal-based account scoring**: A basic 1-10 point model — each positive qualifying signal (e.g., "raised funding in last 6 months") adds points (e.g., +2). A resulting score of 7-10 = reach out immediately; 4-6 = nurture (stay warm on LinkedIn, don't pitch yet); 1-3 = skip/avoid. Yogesh's caveat: signals are a **qualification aid, not a buying predictor** — he's seen zero-signal accounts buy readily and high-signal accounts go nowhere; don't over-trust the score.

**Signal recency windows and the "loosen to get volume" trade-off**: Standard windows: funding signals ~3 months (not 6); "open roles"/hiring signals should exclude postings that are too old (e.g., 6-7+ months). Tightening every signal's time window shrinks the qualifying account pool sharply (e.g., 100 accounts down to 5-10), so in practice you loosen windows (e.g., 3→6 months) to preserve volume, accepting that signals are secondary to overall account fit — there is no formal "decay to zero" mechanism; it's a judgment trade-off between strictness and list size (this directly answered Gagan's question about score decay).

**Data-provider vs. Clay division of labor**: Prospeo/Apollo increasingly ship their own "signal" filters (funding, LinkedIn activity, open roles, expansion) — use them directly when their data proves accurate, because Clay is expensive and everyone tries to minimize Clay credit usage. The one clear exception is **technographic data** (what software/stack a company uses, e.g., Salesforce) — Clay's technographic filtering is meaningfully better because Clay integrates with **BuiltWith**, a dedicated technographics company; Apollo/Prospeo's technographic filters are comparatively weak. (Advanced/future topic: you can pipe Apollo's and Prospeo's own APIs directly into Clay instead of manually exporting — not covered in depth yet.)

**Messaging discipline — signals inform, never announce**: A real mistake Yogesh shared: he emailed a prospect claiming "you raised funding in the last 6 months" based on a Prospeo signal; the recipient replied that they personally never raised funding — the funding belonged to a subsidiary they'd acquired, and re-running the enrichment in Clay confirmed the Prospeo data was wrong at the parent/subsidiary level. Lesson: (1) verify signals are actually true before using them in copy, and (2) even when true, never parrot the raw fact back to the prospect (his analogy: don't email someone "congrats on your marriage, buy a honeymoon package" — it reads as creepy/robotic even if accurate). Instead, signals should shape the *angle* of the pitch without stating the data point directly — e.g., instead of "we saw you're hiring 10 salespeople," say "we saw your sales team is expanding in Delhi — we already work with companies like LambdaTest and ServiceNow there, and given your recent product expansion we think we could help." This requires accumulated sales judgment, which Yogesh says gets easier with repetition.

## Instructor rules, opinions & decisions
- Auto Run must stay OFF on every table except true webhook/live tables, for a minimum of 4-6 months while still learning.
- Never delete a column in Clay for at least 3 months — always Hide/Unhide; deleted data is expensive or impossible to recover.
- Never search for companies natively inside Clay — it's scraped LinkedIn data full of fake company profiles; always originate accounts from a validated provider (Apollo/Prospeo).
- Person-level data in Clay is more trustworthy than company-level data in Clay, because a person can't as easily fabricate an entire fake employer relationship at scale the way fake "companies" get created on LinkedIn (though individual misrepresentation, per D11-D12, still happens).
- Prefer Prospeo/Apollo's native signal filters over Clay's equivalent when the provider's data proves accurate, to save Clay credits — except for technographics, where Clay (via BuiltWith) is always superior.
- Test every new enrichment/prompt on 10 rows before running on the full list.
- Only trust green (high-confidence) AI outputs; treat orange as usable-with-caution and red as unusable.
- Rename generic "Use AI" columns to descriptive names immediately after building them.
- Never let outreach messaging directly recite a signal/data point back to the prospect — signals should shape tone and angle, not be quoted.
- Verify a signal is actually true (re-run/check in Clay) before building messaging around it — provider data can misattribute events like funding rounds to the wrong entity (parent vs. subsidiary).
- Clay is a tool to execute the strategy, not the point of the work — "we don't need to go into Clay like Clay's some revolution"; keep the strategy document as the source of truth and Clay as the execution layer only.
- This session's Clay coverage was intentionally kept to roughly the first 5% of Clay's actual capability, to avoid overwhelming beginners.

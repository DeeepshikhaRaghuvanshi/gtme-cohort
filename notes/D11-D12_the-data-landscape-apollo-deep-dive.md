# D11-D12: The Data Landscape + Apollo Deep Dive (2026-09-15)

## TL;DR
- Before building any list, always ask: why are we building it (cold outbound / event / webinar / partnership), and is this a fresh or repeat reach-out (repeat = reuse and re-enrich existing sheet, don't redownload).
- Decide **People First** (job title/city given) vs. **Company First** (specific companies/employee threshold given) before opening any tool — getting this wrong wastes a lot of downstream work.
- People-first lists must always include a Sales Navigator export because Apollo/Prospeo location data lags real LinkedIn updates by up to 30 days; never rely on a single data provider — use 3+ (Apollo, Prospeo, Sales Navigator).
- Combine-and-dedupe across providers happens in Claude (never Clay, never other LLMs) using a strict hierarchy: LinkedIn URL → email ID → full name + company name; keep the single best available work email.
- QA every list for false profiles: flag people whose LinkedIn-listed company doesn't match their actual work-email domain (common with Infosys/TCS/Qualcomm contractors claiming Google/Meta/Nvidia on LinkedIn).

## Key concepts
**People-first vs. company-first search**: If the task specifies a job title/persona and a city ("find chief lending officers in New York"), it's people-first — search people filters first. If it names companies or a firmographic threshold ("find CEOs of all 10,000+ employee AI companies"), it's company-first — search company filters first. Yogesh calls getting this wrong "expensive" because a lot of downstream filtering work follows from the wrong starting point.

**SLA and delivery expectations**: Data/list-building tasks are same-day SLA. A simple build: under 2 hours. A complex build: 6-8 hours. ClickUp is the standard project-management tool where these tasks get assigned in agency settings.

**Pre-build discovery questions** (before touching any tool): (1) why is this list being built — cold outbound, event invite, webinar invite, partnership reach-out, advisor reach-out; (2) is this a fresh reach-out or a repeat (if repeat with no response, reuse/enrich the existing sheet instead of a new pull); (3) location semantics — does "reach out to Germany" mean person-location Germany, company-headquarters Germany, or both (always clarify with the client); (4) if for LinkedIn, narrow harder because of LinkedIn's monthly connection cap (see below); if for email, volume can stay high since email capacity is effectively unlimited.

**Seniority-level filtering discipline**: A job title like "chief lending officer" already encodes a seniority level (C-suite) — don't double-count or miss that when setting the seniority filter. Standard buyer seniority hierarchy: Director → Head → VP → C-suite are the decision-makers; managers rarely buy. Apollo's own internal seniority ranking (in its UI) counterintuitively places Head *below* Director — don't confuse this UI ordering with real-world seniority. Always exclude "Partner" from decision-maker searches by default — in most departments "Partner" filters surface channel/enablement/talent-partner roles (e.g., someone doing "partner enablement" for channel partners) rather than actual buyers; the exception is context-dependent (e.g., law/consulting firms), so if partners dominate a result set, export them separately to a sheet and confirm with the client rather than guessing.

**Employee-count filtering**: Always exclude 1-10 employee companies — they typically lack budget for GTM/software services. 11-20 is a borderline/still-immature segment.

**LinkedIn's outreach ceiling**: LinkedIn outreach is capped at roughly 700 connections/month per profile, so people-first lists destined for LinkedIn must be narrowed hard (drop lower seniorities, drop small companies) to concentrate outreach on true decision-makers. Email has no such ceiling, so wider persona/company nets are fine for email-only lists.

**Location freshness gap — why Sales Navigator is mandatory for people-first lists**: Apollo/Prospeo scrape and reprocess LinkedIn data with up to a 30-day lag; when someone updates their LinkedIn profile after moving cities, Sales Navigator reflects it almost immediately while Apollo/Prospeo may still show the old city for weeks. Gagan cited Sales Navigator as ~92% accurate for current employer/location. A known limitation: some people (mostly junior roles) set their LinkedIn location to a headquarters country (e.g., "US") rather than their real physical location; senior people (C-suite/VP/director) rarely do this because they aren't monitored the same way. Mitigations discussed: run outreach in the target country's night hours (fewer wrong-timezone responses), block landing/booking pages from opening in unauthorized countries, and accept ~5% of profiles will be "false" location.

**Data-provider distinction — Clay is not a data source**: Clay is an orchestration/enrichment platform, not a data repository. Apollo and Prospeo are "irreplaceable" because they've accumulated validated company/person data over 10+ years and actively clean/validate it. Clay (and any scrape-based tool) can surface false profiles — e.g., a contractor at Infosys who lists "Google" as employer on LinkedIn (because Infosys's client is Google) will show up in Clay/Sales-Navigator scrapes but Apollo/Prospeo's validation would exclude them. This is why a company-name-vs-email-domain QA check (built as a Claude skill) is required after enrichment: flag any profile where the person's work-email domain doesn't match their listed company's domain.

**TAM/SAM/SOM sequencing**: SOM/real conversion parameters can only be learned from live market experimentation after launch — you can't filter your way to SOM in a tool before going live; TAM/SAM come first, SOM is discovered through outreach results (this directly answered one of Deepshikha's questions).

## Tools shown & how they were used
- **Prospeo — people-first search walkthrough (Castle/chief lending officer case study)** (00:22:25–00:31:13): Searched "chief lending officer" + city = New York → only 77 results (insufficient for an in-person dinner event) → widened to entire US → 1,400 raw, narrowed to seniority=C-suite only (excluding owner/founder) → 1,363 → applied "people per company" = 1 to dedupe to unique companies → excluded 1-10 employee companies → final: 1,286 chief lending officers across 1,146 unique companies. Then re-ran with broader department = "lending" (instead of exact title) to surface the next seniority down (VP of Lending): New York alone yielded 773 VPs, narrowed by excluding "AP" (assistant-type roles) and applying per-company=1 → 282 unique people.
- **Prospeo — department-based filtering shortcut** (01:02:49–01:04:20): Instead of stacking multiple job-title keywords, select the "Department" filter (e.g., "Artificial Intelligence" or "Product Marketing") plus location and seniority — much faster than manually combining many title strings, with an "Exclude Department" option (e.g., exclude "Advertising" when hunting product marketing).
- **Prospeo — Daxter (AI-native data ops platform) case study** (00:35:55–00:40:19): Task = "reach out to Daxter personas (data leaders, director+) in Germany." Set person-location = Germany, company-location = Germany (clarify with client whether both must match), search department = "data," seniority = director+, exclude "Partner." Got ~4,152/4,030 contacts after removing 1-10 employee companies — fine for email. For LinkedIn use, narrowed further (removed director/head, kept VP+C-suite) down to 366, given the 700/month LinkedIn connection cap.
- **Apollo — seniority level filter idiosyncrasy** (01:04:20 area): Demonstrated that Apollo's seniority filter list shows "Head" ranked below "Director" in its UI ordering, which doesn't match real-world seniority — a trap to watch for.
- **Apollo — Partner seniority / exclusion example**: Showed a "Partner" filter result including a "Talent Partner" (channel-partner/partnership role) to illustrate why Partner should usually be excluded from buyer-persona lists.
- **Google Sheets — data hygiene / column structure ("Phoenix dinner list" example)** (00:42:49–00:50:48): Mandatory columns for any people-first list: First Name, Last Name, Full Name, Clean First Name (single word, capitalized, safe as an email placeholder — e.g., "Deep" not "Deepshikha ma'am"), Job Title, LinkedIn URL, Cleaned Company Name (placeholder-safe, e.g., "SBI" not "State Bank of India Mobile Branch"), Company Domain, and City/State/Country as **separate columns** (never a single combined location field). Also demoed using Sheets' text filter ("text contains...") to strip irrelevant rows (e.g., keep only rows containing "operating" when hunting COOs) and using a simple "per company" unique count to check how many distinct companies remain.
- **Claude — combine & dedupe process** (01:11:13–01:19:48): After exporting from Apollo + Prospeo + Sales Navigator as separate CSVs, feed them into Claude with a prompt instructing it to: merge all three lists, deduplicate using the hierarchy LinkedIn URL → Email ID → Full Name + Company Name (never dedupe on name alone — risk of same-name collisions), and keep only the single best available work email in one column (Apollo can return personal + work email; Prospeo returns work email only — never use a personal email for outreach). This combine/dedupe step is explicitly never done in Clay or other LLMs (GPT/Grok/Gemini) — always Claude, before the clean list moves to Clay.
- **RB2B**: Mentioned as a website-visitor identification tool restricted to the US market due to EU privacy/compliance restrictions on person-level visitor tracking.
- **n8n**: Reserved for enterprise clients with budget/technical teams; startup/small-client builds should stay manual (not worth building n8n automation for smaller accounts).

## Instructor rules, opinions & decisions
- Never build a list without first asking why it's being built and whether it's a fresh or repeat reach-out.
- Never confuse people-first and company-first search — get this right before any filtering; mistakes here are costly.
- Always exclude 1-10 employee companies (no budget) from list builds.
- Always use Sales Navigator for any people-first list — non-negotiable, because of the 30-day Apollo/Prospeo location lag.
- Never rely on a single data source; always use 3+ providers (this is also why agencies with 8-10 data tool subscriptions outcompete solo GTM engineers).
- Combine & dedupe always happens in Claude, never in Clay or other LLMs — Clay makes this step "complicated"; clean the data before Clay, not inside Clay.
- Dedupe hierarchy is fixed: LinkedIn URL → Email ID → Full Name + Company. Never dedupe on name alone.
- Always run the company-name-vs-email-domain QA check to catch false LinkedIn profiles (contractors misrepresenting employer).
- Never include nonprofits in TAM.
- Exclude "Partner" from buyer-persona searches by default; verify with the client rather than assuming if a large partner segment appears.
- Always separate City/State/Country into distinct columns and produce placeholder-safe "clean" first name and company name columns — described as "top-notch data hygiene" that clients notice.
- List building strategy (which departments/personas to target) is the client's/agency's call, not the GTM engineer's unilateral decision — you execute against their stated requirement, though you should push back with data-driven pushback (e.g., "New York only has 76 people, we need to widen to all of the US") rather than silently deliver an inadequate list.

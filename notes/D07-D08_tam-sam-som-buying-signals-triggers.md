# D07-D08: TAM / SAM / SOM + Buying signals & triggers (2026-09-10)

## TL;DR
- TAM = every company that could theoretically buy (the whole ICP universe); SAM = the subset you can actually serve given geography/product/compliance/pricing limits; SOM = the specific slice you'll realistically go after right now, narrowed further by market conditions (competition, pricing sensitivity, compliance) — not just another filter.
- Worked live in Apollo on a real portfolio company (Below AI, an AI-native finance/accounting firm): TAM = 151,000 companies (US/Canada/Europe, 50-5,000 employees, healthcare-adjacent industries) → SAM = 54,000 companies (100-5,000 employees, narrowed industries, US-only) → ICP contact list = 184 people from a 25-company sample (finance/accounting titles, Director-to-C-suite).
- Every strategy document must include **screenshots** of the actual Apollo/Prospio/Exa filters used for TAM, SAM, and ICP — clients (especially US/European ones) want evidence, not verbal claims.
- Signals = public, account-level information (funding, hiring, LinkedIn posts) scored on a 1-10 "account scoring" scale; Intent = person-level behavioral engagement (visiting your pricing page, liking a founder's post); Trigger = the outreach action you take in response.
- SOM doesn't get its own database filter — it's a narrower subset of SAM justified by a written explanation of market conditions (e.g., "SAM includes US+Europe, but we're focusing on US first because Europe has compliance overhead and India-like markets are price-sensitive").

## Key concepts

**TAM, defined with the HubSpot example.** If HubSpot could theoretically sell to every business needing a CRM anywhere in the world, that entire universe is its TAM — aspirational/total, not filtered by current capability.

**SAM, defined.** The portion of TAM a company can actually serve given real constraints — compliance, pricing structure, localization. HubSpot's SAM might realistically be US, Europe, and parts of Asia — not global — due to language barriers, compliance gaps, or unready infrastructure elsewhere.

**SOM, defined and distinguished from SAM.** SOM isn't found via another database filter — it's a **written, reasoned narrowing** of SAM based on qualitative conditions: pricing sensitivity (India, where Zoho/local players undercut price), competitive density, compliance burden (Europe's data regulations). HubSpot's SAM might be US+Europe+Asia, but its SOM narrows to the US because India is price-sensitive and Europe needs heavier compliance work. SOM is "a component of SAM" tied to **short-term traction goals** — this is where a GTM engineer's day-to-day targeting actually lives, since early-stage companies need obtainable wins fast, not a five-year plan.

**Live case study #1 — Lightfield (Alok's company, AI legal-ops for in-house counsel).** TAM = all companies with in-house legal teams (~145,000 in-house counsel roles in the US alone). SAM = narrowed to 5+ in-house legal professionals, substantial recurring legal workload, modern infrastructure, AI-adoption willingness (~10,000-20,000 companies). SOM = US-based, tech-forward, 100-5,000 employees, 5-30+ legal professionals, high contract-request volume, modern collaboration stack — justified because Lightfield's actual product build constrains who it can serve well right now, and because most GTM engineering happens at exactly this early, pre-PMF stage.

**Live case study #2 — Below AI (Medha's company, AI-native finance/accounting), built step-by-step in Apollo.** Problem framing: Below automates recurring finance workflows, so best-fit prospects have complex monthly closes or large accounting teams. TAM: Location = US+Canada+Europe, Employees = 50-5,000, industries broadened to biotech/pharma/medical devices/life sciences/healthcare research/health tech/diagnostics/clinical research/hospital & healthcare (chosen because Below's real customers cluster there) → **151,000 companies**. SAM: narrow Employees to 100-5,000, keep only healthcare-adjacent industries, narrow Location to US-only → **54,000 companies**. ICP: from a 25-company sample, Job Title = finance/accounting/controller, Management Level = Director-to-C-suite → **184 people**, framed as "Phase 1" of a multi-phase plan, not the whole campaign.

**Why screenshots are mandatory.** American/European clients "don't trust words" — a claim like "Below can sell to 200,000 companies" needs an actual filtered screenshot from Apollo/Prospio/Exa. Every strategy document needs three screenshot sets: TAM, SAM, and ICP filters, across all three data tools.

**Per-industry / per-persona micro-campaigns (not mass blasts).** Even with a broad multi-industry SAM, pick one industry at a time (e.g., biotech), split into decision-makers vs. champions, and run **decision-makers on LinkedIn, champions via email** as separate campaigns. If one combination shows strong response, double down rather than diversifying immediately. Contrast: pre-GTM-engineering era ran one mass campaign to millions; today's discipline is many small, hypothesis-tested micro-campaigns.

**Market response is empirical, not fully predictable.** Analogies used: Swiggy vs. Zomato (near-identical, but Swiggy wins tier-1 cities, Zomato tier-2/3) and Zudio vs. H&M (Zudio outperforms in tier-2/3 despite being less premium) — founders build on conviction, but which segment responds is discovered empirically after launch, which is the research/feedback role a GTM engineer plays (Below's healthcare traction became the reason to concentrate TAM/SAM there).

**Signals — a live "account scoring" walkthrough.** A signal is public, account-level information building confidence a company is ready to buy. Live additive exercise with Medha on a hypothetical Below prospect: employee posts "AI can automate finance/accounting" (+2, score=2) → company raised ₹10 crore (+2, score=4) → company hiring 50 finance/accounting people (+2, score=6). This **account scoring** runs 1-10; a company scoring 6+ from stacked signals is a stronger near-term opportunity than one with none.

**Intent — person-level, distinct from signal.** Intent is an individual's behavioral engagement: visiting your website/pricing page, liking a founder's post. "Meta will not visit your profile; a person who works at Meta will visit" — intent always resolves to a person, not the company abstraction.

**Trigger — the outreach action.** The response to an intent signal — e.g., pricing-page visit → "we saw you checking pricing, would you like help?"; FAQ engagement → address likely objections directly. Workflow: build signals → detect intent → convert into a trigger.

## Instructor rules, opinions & decisions
- Never present TAM/SAM numbers to a client as a verbal claim — always back them with an actual filtered screenshot from the data tool.
- Never blast one massive campaign across an entire SAM — always break into per-industry, per-persona micro-campaigns, and double down on whichever specific combination shows response.
- Decision-makers get LinkedIn outreach; champions get email outreach (as the default channel split within a segment).
- SOM requires no additional tool filter — it requires a written justification of market conditions (pricing sensitivity, competition, compliance) explaining why you're focusing on a subset of SAM right now.
- Every account/lead in a GTM engineering system should ultimately carry TAM/SAM/SOM classification plus its signal data — this is described as standard practice, to be built out further in later sessions.
- Choose a company you understand or are genuinely interested in for the ongoing coursework project — Yogesh's own preference is insurance companies; he noted that GTM engineers/agencies who specialize in one niche (e.g., cybersecurity) progress faster and earn more than generalists.
- A YC company not yet clear on its own exact product focus is still a fine (even ideal) choice for this exercise — early-stage ambiguity is normal, and YC companies are especially good practice targets because they avoid bloated headcount and are open to working with lean GTM help.
- GTM engineering client relationships are framed as multi-year: companies get roughly 1-2 years to figure out their GTM approach, so a good GTM engineer who nails the early research typically retains that client for a long stretch.
- Deadline given: have the GTM strategy document (through TAM/SAM/SOM/ICP/signals) ready by the following Monday, understanding more topics will still be layered on afterward.
